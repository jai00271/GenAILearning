EM 610 · Culture, Psychological Safety & Inclusion

Safety slogans nahi — dissent, learning, aur Mutual Benefit norms

Track: EM · Phase 2 Audience: Technical Manager → Engineering Manager Prereq: EM 609 GenAI Eng Teams Next: EM 611 Incident Leadership

Jai / Mritunjai jaisi TM journey mein culture often “soft topic” lagta hai — jab tak GenAI ship pe golden-set miss, cost spike, ya identity-adjacent hallucination aata hai aur koi bolne se darta hai. EM 610 ka job: psychological safety ko Amy Edmondson ke research sense mein samajhna (paraphrase + cite, copy nahi), inclusion ko hiring slogan se alag rakhna, aur Koch-style MBM Principle of Mutual Benefit ko team norms mein translate karna.

Apple/Walmart-class EM loops pe “tell me about a time someone disagreed with you” aur “how do you run blameless postmortems” common hain. Yeh lesson un answers ke operating system pe focused hai — incidents ka mechanics EM 611 mein.

Recall · EM 609 · EM 603

EM 609 ne eval ownership aur platform/app split rakha. EM 603 ne coaching scripts diye. Safety ke bina eval owner “bad news” hide karega; coaching ke bina dissent personal attack ban jayega. Culture = woh glue jo staffing + feedback ko honest rakhti hai.

1. Psychological safety — paraphrase, not poster

Edmondson ka core idea (paraphrased): team members believe they can speak up about mistakes, questions, and concerns without interpersonal punishment. Yeh “being nice” nahi hai — yeh learning behavior enable karti hai: ask for help, admit uncertainty, challenge a plan. Google’s Project Aristotle / re:Work materials bhi team effectiveness mein psychological safety ko central factor ke tor pe discuss karte hain — detail ke liye unke public pages padho, yahan slogan paste nahi.

GenAI context: junior engineer ko kehna chahiye “golden set pe regression aa gayi” bina fear ke ke “ship date todd diya”. Backend-strong IC ko kehna chahiye “vendor eval opaque hai” bina “anti-innovation” label ke.

2. Inclusion that is not a slogan wall

Inclusion EM ke liye operational hai: meeting airtime, design-review invitations, on-call rotation fairness, language that doesn’t gatekeep (“real ML people only”), aur hiring loops jo accent/school prestige pe shadow-score na karein. Poster pe “diversity” likhna cheap hai; retro mein quiet voices ko explicitly invite karna expensive — aur valuable.

~5-person Koch team reality: agar ek hi “GenAI person” hai, unka overload + hero culture inclusion fail hai. Spread literacy (office hours, pairing) taaki identity / .NET veterans bhi eval language bol saken — EM 609 pairing theme yahan culture ban jati hai.

Director lens

Directors ask: “Who spoke last staff? Who owns bad news?” Agar same two names, safety/inclusion theater hai. Jai-style answer: named facilitation habits + documented decision logs (EM 607) + eval dashboards that anyone can challenge.

3. Dissent as a product feature

Healthy dissent protocols: (1) steelman the proposal first, (2) name the risk in user/cost/compliance terms, (3) propose a cheaper test, (4) time-box the disagreement. EM ka role: dissent ko personality fight se nikaal ke decision record mein daalna. “I disagree and commit” tabhi ethical hai jab dissent actually heard ho.

4. Blameless culture — and its limits

Blameless postmortems (SRE tradition) systems pe focus karte hain: latent conditions, missing alerts, unclear ownership. Limit yeh hai: willful negligence, harassment, ya repeated reckless bypass of known gates “blameless” umbrella ke neeche nahi aate. EM clearly bole: learning vs accountability tracks alag ho sakte hain — dono honest.

Pitfall

“Blameless” ko “consequences zero” mat banao. Doosra pitfall: safety theater — retros mein sticky notes, lekin jo engineer cost spike raise kare usko “not a team player” whisper. Measure speak-up by what changed after bad news, not by posters.

5. MBM Mutual Benefit → team norms

Koch’s Market-Based Management emphasizes Principle of Mutual Benefit: long-term relationships where value is created for customers, employees, and the company — not zero-sum heroics. Team norms draft (example, adapt — don’t invent company policy): (a) ship gates beat demo applause, (b) credit shared on ServiceNow / identity automation wins, (c) challenge ideas early, (d) no surprise exec decks — bad news travels faster than good.

Interview narrative tip (EM 612 preview): jab aap ~95% on-time delivery claim mention karo, frame as your stated operating claim to verify with evidence, aur culture angle add karo — on-time pressure ne safety ko crush kiya ya nahi? Fake metrics mat ghado; STAR mein process + trade-off dikhao.

Hands-on · 30-min norms workshop

Apni 5-person team ke saath 5 norms draft karo (1 page). Har norm ke saath ek anti-pattern likho (“what we will not do”). Include one GenAI-specific norm (e.g. “no production prompt change without eval note”). Share in next 1:1.

Self-check · formative only

1. Psychological safety ka practical meaning?

Reveal answer

Belief that speak-up (misses, questions, dissent) won’t bring interpersonal punishment — enables learning, not “be soft on quality.”

2. Inclusion slogan se kaise alag?

Reveal answer

Airtime, review access, on-call fairness, anti-gatekeeping language, fair loops — behaviors + systems, not wall posters.

3. Blameless culture ki limit?

Reveal answer

Systems learning yes; willful harm / harassment / reckless gate-bypass still need accountability tracks.

4. Mutual Benefit → team norm example?

Reveal answer

e.g. bad news faster than good; eval gates beat demo applause; shared credit on automation wins — customer/employee/org value, not heroics.
