EM 601 · Role Shift · Phase 2 Leadership

Title pe “Manager” pehle se hai — EM bar alag hai: org outcomes, hiring ownership, performance authority

Track: EM Audience: Technical Manager → Engineering Manager Prereq: Phase 1 FOUND→CAP (in progress / complete) Next: EM 602 · EM Operating System

Jai — aap already Technical Manager ho Koch Global Services pe: ~5 engineers, ASP.NET Core / microservices / RabbitMQ / identity (SAML/OAuth) background, delivery + mentoring, MBM principles ke saath. Yeh chapter “team kya hoti hai” nahi sikhata. Yeh delta sikhata hai: Technical Manager se Engineering Manager tak — especially Apple / Walmart-class orgs jahan EM ka job description sirf sprint velocity nahi, balki people system + hiring bar + multi-stakeholder outcomes hota hai.

Phase 1 (FOUND→CAP) ne aapko GenAI product judgment diya. Phase 2 EM track us judgment ko org leverage mein badalta hai: aap khud model call nahi karte — aap wo system banate ho jisme log reliably ship karte hain, grow karte hain, aur bar raise karte hain.

Recall · aapka starting point

Aap pehle se lead karte ho: 1:1s, mentoring, delivery ownership, MBM framing. EM 601 assume karta hai yeh muscles maujood hain. Naya kaam: scope expand — hiring loop ownership, performance decisions with real authority, product/process tradeoffs across stakeholders, aur “main last PR review karun” se nikalna.

1. Ladder clear karo: IC · Tech Lead · Technical Manager · EM

Industry titles messy hain. Same word alag companies mein alag mean karta hai. Interview / promotion conversation mein aapko scope describe karna chahiye, title nahi. Yeh frame use karo:

Role Primary output People scope Decision authority (typical)
Senior / Staff IC Design + code + technical direction on problems Influence peers; no direct reports Technical tradeoffs in owned area
Tech Lead Delivery quality + architecture coherence for a squad Leads without formal HR authority (often) Tech path, sequencing; hiring input only
Technical Manager Team delivery + mentoring + local process Direct reports (small); still deep in tech Local people guidance; limited hiring / perf formal power
Engineering Manager Org outcomes via people system Owns hiring, growth, performance, capacity Hire / no-hire weight, perf ratings, roadmap capacity calls

Aapka aaj ka role TM ke paas baithta hai: aap already people lead karte ho, lekin enterprise EM bar yeh puchhta hai — kya aap org ke liye bar raise kar sakte ho jab aap coding hero nahi rahoge?

Director lens · Apple / Walmart-class

Hiring director yeh nahi puchhta “kitne microservices aapne likhe?”. Woh puchhta hai: (1) aapne kab kisi ko promote / PIP / exit ke liye clear call liya? (2) hiring mein aapka bar raise example kya hai? (3) jab product, security, aur platform clash hue, aapne capacity kaise defend ki? Agar answers sirf “team happy + sprints green” hain — EM bar incomplete.

2. Teen buckets: People · Process · Product

EM ka week in teen buckets mein split hota hai. TM aksar Product+Process pe overweight rehta hai, People pe mentoring style — lekin formal people system (scorecards, calibration, career ladders) thin rehta hai.

  • People: hiring, 1:1s, feedback, growth plans, performance, psychological safety, succession.
  • Process: rituals, incident learning, estimation honesty, dependency management, quality gates (yahan GenAI eval mindset bhi aata hai).
  • Product / tech outcomes: roadmap capacity, risk calls, “build vs buy”, when to say no to a shiny GenAI feature without eval.

Rule of thumb for early EM (5–8 reports): calendar pe intentionally ~40–50% people, ~25–30% process/stakeholder, ~20–30% product/tech judgment — aur coding time intentionally shrink. Agar aap ab bhi 60% coding hero ho, aap TM mode mein stuck ho.

Pitfall · hero manager

“Main weekend pe RabbitMQ consumer fix kar deta hoon” short-term delivery bachaata hai, long-term team ko signal deta hai: tough work manager ke paas jaati hai. Enterprise EM interviews mein yeh anti-pattern openly flag hota hai. Fix: pair / coach, ownership transfer, phir aap observability pe review — implementation pe nahi.

3. Authority vs influence — dono chahiye, mix alag hai

Tech Lead mostly influence se chalta hai. EM ke paas formal authority aati hai: hiring vote weight, performance rating, headcount asks, vendor / tooling budget influence. Magar authority se culture nahi banti — influence se banti hai. Best EMs authority ko quietly use karte hain, roz-roz influence se lead karte hain.

Practical test: agar aapki title hata di jaye, kya log ab bhi aapki judgment follow karein? Agar haan — influence strong hai. Agar sirf title se kaam hota hai — aap compliance manager ho, EM nahi.

4. Koch MBM → enterprise EM translation

Market-Based Management (MBM) aapke current context ka strength hai: decision rights, value creation, challenge culture. Enterprise EM interviews mein MBM jargon dump mat karo — behaviors translate karo:

MBM-flavored habit Enterprise EM language Story prompt for interviews
Decision rights clarity RACI / DRI ownership on tech + people decisions “Kab maine explicitly decide kiya vs facilitate kiya?”
Challenge / constructive debate Psychological safety + disagree-and-commit “Kab junior ne mujhe wrong prove kiya aur main celebrate kiya?”
Value creation focus Outcome metrics over activity metrics “Kab maine a feature kill kiya kyunki customer value thin thi?”
Knowledge sharing Written RFCs, runbooks, hiring scorecards “Kaise maine tacit knowledge ko team asset banaya?”
Hands-on · MBM → EM one-pager

Ek page likho (private): 4 columns — MBM principle · concrete Koch example · enterprise EM phrasing · evidence metric. Interview prep ke liye yeh aapka translation sheet banega. Jargon-free English draft bhi rakho.

5. Time split: maker schedule chhodo, manager schedule design karo

Paul Graham wala maker-vs-manager tension aapke liye real hai — 14 years IC muscle strong hai. EM week intentionally fragmented hota hai: 1:1s, hiring, incident bridges, stakeholder syncs. Guilt mat feel karo fragmented calendar pe — guilt feel karo agar aapne reports ke deep-work blocks protect nahi kiye.

  • Apna deep tech time: 1–2 protected blocks / week (architecture review, spike), daily hero coding nahi.
  • Reports ke liye: meeting-free mornings ya “no-meeting Wednesdays” negotiate karo.
  • Hiring weeks pe coding almost zero — yeh normal hai, apologize mat karo.

6. Multi-stakeholder EM — aap ab sirf “dev team ka manager” nahi

Large-org EM regularly negotiate karta hai:

  • Product: scope cuts, sequencing, “GenAI demo” vs production bar.
  • Security / Identity: aapka SAML/OAuth background yahan trust capital hai — EM role mein aap translator bante ho, implementer nahi.
  • Platform / SRE: SLOs, on-call load, rabbitMQ / queue debt.
  • HR / Recruiting: headcount, leveling, offer calibration.
  • Peer EMs: shared services, dependency contracts, hiring bar consistency.

Script (stakeholder pushback): “Hum yeh feature quarter mein le sakte hain agar X cut ho, ya Y risk accept ho. Meri recommendation: ship thinner slice with eval gate, full vision next quarter. Main capacity number ke saath aaunga Thursday.” — emotion nahi, options + recommendation + date.

7. Common failure modes: TM → EM transition

  1. Shadow IC: har design decision personally ratify; team atrophy.
  2. Friendship trap: hard feedback delay; performance debt accumulate.
  3. Meeting sponge: calendar open; no strategy blocks; reactive forever.
  4. Hiring as favor: “known good person” without scorecard → bar drift.
  5. MBM / values lecture without systems: posters yes, rituals no.
  6. GenAI FOMO leadership: pilots without eval — CAP track ke lessons ignore karke team ko demo theater mein dhakelna.
Pitfall · “main technical rahunga”

Identity threat real hai: 14 saal engineering pride. EM banne ka matlab technical judgment chorna nahi — implementation monopoly chorna hai. Aap ab architecture reviews, hiring tech screens, and risk calls se technical rehte ho. Pride shift: “maine fix kiya” → “team ne bina meri night ke ship kiya.”

8. 90-day self-contract (Koch pe practice, interview pe story)

Aapko naya job milne se pehle bhi EM muscles practice kar sakte ho:

  • Days 1–30: Written role charter for yourself — people / process / product % targets; stop-doing list (which PR types you will not own).
  • Days 31–60: Hiring scorecard draft for next open role (even if informal); one structured debrief after an interview.
  • Days 61–90: One growth plan co-written with a report; one difficult feedback delivered with SBI (EM 603); calendar audit published to yourself weekly.
Director lens · story quality

Strong EM candidates concrete numbers + decisions laate hain: “5 engineers, reduced P1 escape rate from X→Y by changing on-call + review ritual,” “hired 2 / rejected 7 against scorecard,” “said no to a GenAI chatbot without golden-set eval.” Vague “I mentored the team” stories die in panel.

Check yourself

1. Tech Lead aur EM mein sabse bada structural farq kya hai?

Reveal answer

Formal people-system authority: hiring weight, performance decisions, capacity ownership — sirf technical direction nahi.

2. MBM ko enterprise EM interview mein kaise translate karein?

Reveal answer

Jargon dump nahi — behaviors: decision rights → DRI/RACI; challenge → safety + disagree-and-commit; value creation → outcomes over activity.

3. “Hero manager” failure mode kya signal karta hai?

Reveal answer

Manager critical-path coder bana rehta hai; team ownership atrophies; EM bar (org leverage) miss.

4. Director lens pe teen must-have story types?

Reveal answer

Hard people call (promote/PIP/exit clarity), bar-raising hire/no-hire, multi-stakeholder capacity/risk decision with numbers.
